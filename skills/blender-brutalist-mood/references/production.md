# Concrete monument study

## Structure before texture

Identify function: courtyard, civic stair, atrium or memorial. Describe a load path: slab carried by piers, recess beneath cantilever, stair between retaining walls. Select a dimensioned human-scale cue such as a door. Concrete boxes alone do not establish an inhabitable building.

## Scene recipe

Build three repeated pier bays, a raised slab and one deep access recess. A 3–4 m bay is a useful courtyard experiment, then revise it to the actual reference. Do not enlarge every doorway to communicate monumentality. Chamfer exposed edges enough to catch grazing light at the final distance. Keep expansion joints and board-form marks subordinate to massing and independently adjustable.

Start nonmetal concrete around roughness 0.7–0.9 with broad aggregate variation and weaker fine bump. Scale board patterns in world units and inspect a side-lit crop. Overcast illumination emphasizes planar hierarchy; a low sun emphasizes recess rhythm. Pick the one fitting the brief. A moderately wide camera with controlled verticals conveys occupancy; an extreme low wide lens may caricature it. Keep a neutral grade until structure and scale read.

## Repair and evidence

Toy scale calls for checking doors, stair rise and camera height against the scale cue before adding detail. Rock-like concrete needs lower bump amplitude and clear construction joints. Remove incidental ruin or colored fog if the requested building is clean.

Require a neutral massing image, the final human-scale view and a raking-light material crop. Accept when supports, access and scale are intelligible, texture frequency fits distance and repetition produces the intended austerity. This visual study does not certify structural engineering.

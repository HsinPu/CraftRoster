# Playtesting and iteration

## Design an informative test

Convert the uncertainty into a player-level hypothesis: what the participant should notice, predict, attempt, choose or experience. Identify a plausible observation that would contradict it. Choose a prototype, paper exercise, build or interview that can expose that failure; explain what it cannot answer. A paper economy can expose arithmetic and comprehension issues without establishing real-time combat pacing.

Record the exact build or artifact revision, starting conditions and requested participant task. Recruit from the relevant audience and state limitations such as colleagues already familiar with the rules. Set a reasonable session shape from the question and participant needs rather than a universal sample size or duration. Use consent for participation/recording and collect only relevant information.

Give neutral instructions. When testing discovery, do not demonstrate the intended solution; define when the facilitator may help and log any assistance. A critical defect may require stopping or bypassing a step, but the resulting downstream observations must retain that context.

Choose observations that bear on the hypothesis:

| Question | Useful observation | Easy false inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the player understand the cost? | Predicted outcome before purchase; observed surprise afterward | A completed purchase proves comprehension |
| Is the risky option meaningful? | When and why it is chosen or avoided, given context | Raw use count proves depth |
| Can players find the route? | Wrong turns, cue visibility, assistance and own explanation | Eventually reaching the goal proves clarity |
| Does progression create new decisions? | Changed strategy after unlock; reasons to continue/stop | More time played proves enjoyment |

Set a decision rule before collecting data where practical. Early thresholds are local working criteria, not industry facts. Include evidence that leaves the result unresolved. A tiny convenience sample is useful for finding problems but cannot establish a population failure rate.

## Read actual results

Separate three layers in notes: observed action, participant interpretation and designer explanation. Use timestamps or event context where available. "Player waited at the gate" is an observation; "player was confused" needs supporting speech or behavior. Neutral follow-ups such as asking what they expected help distinguish an unclear cue from a deliberate choice.

Compare the observed path with the hypothesis. Classify failures as implementation defects, missing/contradictory rules, unreadable feedback or an experience mismatch; they need different remedies. Do not average away a severe dead end. Avoid causal claims from correlations, especially when familiarity, hardware, accessibility needs or facilitator help differ between sessions.

When interpreting telemetry, state event definitions, denominator and missing sessions. A drop after a boss can mean frustration, planned stopping or instrumentation failure; choose a follow-up that distinguishes them. Report individual anecdotes as anecdotes and model outputs as models.

## Iterate

Prioritize the issue that invalidates the main question. Propose the smallest informative change with a predicted observable difference. If two changes interact, test them together deliberately and explain attribution limits; otherwise avoid bundling unrelated redesigns into one retest.

Return build/setup, participant context, observations, interpretation, limitations and next decision in the user's requested format. If no sessions occurred, deliver the protocol, observation sheet and provisional decision rules only. Do not fabricate quotes, participant counts, screenshots, enjoyment, statistical confidence or balance validation.

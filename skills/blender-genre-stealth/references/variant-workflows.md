# Infiltration, surfaces and visibility art workflows

Use the actual game's visibility, sound, stance and access definitions. A dark render is not proof of invisibility; roughness is not an audio simulation. Keep art, concealment/collision proxies and temporary guard/camera cones distinct.

## Shadow-lane infiltration

Build a lit crossing and sheltered perimeter using actual standing/crouched eye/clearance dimensions. Place practical fixtures with visible mounting and a reason for the light pool; include the route's entry and exit in the study. Keep shadow transitions legible without making essential geometry black. A brighter diagnostic should expose all walls, openings and contacts.

Test player/guard-eye samples and both stance heights, with static cone overlays separately. If exposure crushing hides a ledge or corner, repair local material/light separation. If an apparently opaque hedge has open proxy intent, reconcile art and contract instead of asserting safety. Deliver lit/diagnostic views and labeled sample geometry; AI visibility and moving patrol detection remain unverified.

## Social infiltration and access states

When the brief relies on clothing, credentials or public/private space, build thresholds, counters, barriers and staff/service routes rather than simply adding darkness. Define approved visitor/staff/restricted visual states and identifiable access hardware. Keep an ordinary public path readable and show both sides of the checkpoint at human scale.

Use architectural/material hierarchy and role-specific props without assuming one uniform or culture universally signifies permission. If every door appears equally restricted, revise threshold shape/state accents. If a barrier blocks actual staff circulation, repair its opening/placement. Deliver access-state and reverse-view sheet; credentials, authorization and social AI are separate systems, not inferred from props.

## Vertical entry, vents and ledges

Use supplied actor envelope, climb/reach and camera sweep. Build vent opening, internal bend, exit, ladder/ledge or requested hardware as actual volumes, not a small facade grille. Include attachment/fastener and hand/foot contact cues appropriate to the construction. Stowed/open covers need their own swept bounds.

Inspect approach/entry/interior/exit plus a side section. If the vent is too small, enlarge the authorized architecture or mark it decorative—never shrink the actor silently. If the camera sees through missing backs, complete the volume. Deliver dimensions, open/closed states and contact/camera samples. A gauge fit is not proof of crawling, climbing or camera collision.

## Noise-material and distraction-prop vocabulary

Use actual game sound classes to choose distinct visual families such as grates, carpet, standing water or loose debris. Model thickness/edge/contact so materials read from the intended camera, with roughness and shape rather than color alone. Build a distraction prop with intact/used or moving/rest state only as supplied, keeping grip/throw or activation anchor explicit.

If a loud-looking grate is classified silent by the game, report and reconcile the cue rather than changing audio rules. If every bottle/container looks interactable, differentiate decoration/state grammar. Deliver material-state sheet and close/far views, with sound/detection behavior explicitly untested. Static sight cones and material labels are interface proposals, not executed stealth mechanics.

# Friendly design, wrong performance

## Retain original appeal

Record silhouette, brand palette and construction: plush suit, foam puppet, wood marionette or plastic stage figure. Select one discrepancy such as fixed gaze or an over-open jaw. Horror need not mean injury or monster anatomy.

## Physical recipe

Block a readable friendly head/body before damage. Add seams, stuffing compression, hinge spacing or costume entry consistent with material. A zipper implies a wearable shell; wood needs articulation instead. Make eye domes and pupils separately adjustable.

For felt begin near roughness 0.8 with subtle short-fiber normal rather than long universal fur. For plastic eyes compare roughness 0.1–0.2 with a broad reflection. Scale stitches/fibers against head dimensions. Stage a plausible show or maintenance environment and reveal enough of the friendly design to retain contrast. Introduce one small eye-aim mismatch or stiff posture first. A 3-degree gaze offset may be an initial experiment, but judge the actual render rather than treating it as a universal uncanny value.

## Repair and evidence

A generic monster needs original mascot masses restored and mutations reduced. A pristine-toy read needs the selected performance discrepancy before generic grime. Gravel-like felt needs bump frequency/amplitude reduced and checked under a broad reflection.

Require neutral construction, material close-up, final shot and gaze/pose comparison. Accept credible assembly, recognizable appeal and a restrained approved discrepancy. View the clip before claiming uncanny timing in animation; a frozen pose cannot validate performance. Unrequested wounds and gore must not be introduced as a default.

# Mascot construction families

Keep the approved friendly design and selected discrepancy. Choose the physical assembly before introducing wear, gaze mismatch or pose. The following families are alternatives; do not give a wearable plush shell impossible wooden joints merely because both are called puppets.

## Wearable fabric or foam costume

Block exterior silhouette and an internal wearer/clearance proxy when the fiction includes a performer. Place the entry zipper or removable panel where it could plausibly open. Fabric seams, foam inserts and rigid eye/mouth pieces are separate components. Show a maintenance/open state only when requested and keep the normal costume recoverable.

Use broad stuffing/foam compression at neck, shoulders and contact points. Sewn panels guide fiber and seam placement; stains follow handling/storage rather than covering every surface. Light softly enough to preserve the friendly form, with a grazing diagnostic to reveal fabric versus plastic eyes. If the costume becomes a generic monster, restore original mass relationships before changing intensity. Inspect neutral/final, seam/entry and eye close-ups; a still shell does not establish performer fit or ventilation.

## Hand or rod puppet

Define hand/rod access and hinge/fold region before cutting a mouth opening. Separate upper face, jaw and soft connecting fabric so an open mouth has a believable cavity and does not expose backfaces. For a rod-controlled arm, record attachment point and preserve a posing proxy; rods need not be visible in the final if the brief says otherwise.

Use foam/felt broad forms with limited fibers and a separate rigid eye reflection. Choose one performance discrepancy, such as a delayed blink or gaze held past a turn. Static gaze studies can compare matched eye poses; timing claims need actual playback. Repair jaw interpenetration through pivot/soft-region shape rather than hiding it in black. Deliver closed/open jaw and neutral/uncanny comparison with approved expression intact.

## Wood or mechanical stage figure

Build head, jaw, torso and limbs as joined pieces with measurable pivots and gaps. Grain follows each carved piece; painted coatings differ from bare joint surfaces. Plastic/metal mechanisms can be used when the authored design supports them, with clear fasteners and service access.

Test jaw and limb extremes on a duplicate. A fixed smile should be an intentional design feature rather than a failed deformation. If uncanny effect requires a slow head turn, inspect motion plus the held end pose; frame sampling alone misses temporal drift. Repair hinge collisions and dangling pieces before adding grime. Accept believable assembly and the chosen pose/performance discrepancy, while leaving real mechanism function unverified.

## Rigid plastic mascot

Use a molded shell language: parting line, separate inserts, thickness and attachment zones. Broad reflection cards should reveal the original rounded silhouette without turning all materials into chrome. Eye domes, pupils and lids need independently controllable transforms/materials for gaze studies.

If an eye offset merely looks cross-eyed or changes character identity, compare smaller controlled variations and the neutral reference instead of prescribing one universal angle. If the face becomes featureless under reflection, revise light size/direction and roughness before darkening every material. Inspect neutral reflection, gaze comparison and final shot at the same framing. Surface presence is not proof of a production-ready toy or stage device.

## Handoff and evidence

Deliver the original/derived source, construction family, separated eye/jaw controls, material dependencies and exact reviewed images or clip. Preserve approved palette/intensity. Repair material scale and joints before analog/retro treatment; recording noise cannot validate construction. Game behavior and audience fear remain separate from asset acceptance.

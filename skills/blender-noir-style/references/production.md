# Shadows as composition

## Choose the dramatic relation

Identify subject, concealment and light source: a figure near a blind-covered window, a detective under a streetlamp or an alley seen through rain. Monochrome is optional; neo-noir may retain chosen color accents. Wet pavement and smoke are not required for every scene.

## Light and stage

Block silhouette and negative space first. For an office study place an area or sun-like source through actual slats/gobo geometry. Adjust source angular size or distance until shadow edges suit the reference. Keep the pattern off critical facial features when expression must read. Use black cards/occluders or reduced world fill for negative fill, but retain a narrow separation edge on dark clothing against the background.

In a wet-street study separate puddle and dry asphalt masks. Test puddles around roughness 0.08–0.2 and dry road around 0.65, then compare photographed reference. Add small normal variation so reflections are broken rather than perfect floor mirrors. Compose the reflected light as a secondary shape that leads toward the subject. A moderate long lens can compress shadow planes; a wider close view can exaggerate threat. Choose from the intended scene rather than imposing a lens.

## Repair and evidence

Crushed blacks need selective fill/value separation and exposure review before a universal brightening pass. Flat gray noir needs a more purposeful shadow mask, not more grain. A wet road resembling chrome needs dielectric response and puddle breakup.

Require ungraded/final images, a grayscale thumbnail even for color work, and a shadow/reflection diagnostic crop. Accept readable silhouette, coherent motivated shadows and controlled black detail. View actual movement before accepting smoke, flicker or traveling gobo patterns. A filmic grade does not prove correct lighting.

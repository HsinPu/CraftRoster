# Exploration, construction and travel-scale workflows

## Exploration landmark hierarchy

Define bounded study area, eye height/FOV, travel speed, far view and route/POI vocabulary from the project. Build terrain first, then a regional silhouette, subordinate destination and local approach marker at different scales. A four-chunk study should include high/low routes and a return view; compare which landmark remains visible at each sampled point. Use major shape/negative space and terrain framing before colored beacons.

Create region identity through geology, construction and species distribution rather than a uniform tint. Keep neutral/fog-off and intended atmosphere views. If every POI competes, reduce secondary height/contrast or change outline; if the landmark vanishes behind a tree line, revise canopy/approach mass. Deliver sampled-view/landmark table and distance-tier renders. Landmark spacing is a proposed art/layout study, not verified discovery pacing or navigation.

## Terrain chunks and biome borders

Use declared chunk origin, grid, shared-edge coordinates, elevations and material/UV policy. Build straight/corner/height-transition joins with scatter disabled first. Shared edge positions alone do not ensure smooth normals/material continuity: inspect evaluated boundary vertices, shading and texture scale. Keep a high-detail source and derived chunk copies with named export membership.

Transition between biomes through substrate, drainage, exposure and authored species zones; abrupt fictional borders still need continuous geometry. If a seam cracks, repair coordinate/evaluation mismatch rather than hide it with bushes. If textures change scale, reconcile UV/tiling contracts. Deliver boundary sections, edge/normal/material notes and adjacent-chunk images. Blender assembly does not validate streaming, origin rebasing or engine terrain collision.

## Sandbox construction kit

Define module cell, attachment face, origin, outward normal, legal rotations and occupancy/overhang separately. Build floor, wall, corner, opening, roof and one service element using these interfaces. Assemble a small shelter, a rotated connection and an open/removal state; inspect both interior and exterior. Decorative variation must stay within attachment/clearance boundaries or be marked incompatible.

Use material/edge treatment to distinguish connectable faces from decorative surfaces without permanent diagnostic labels. If a rotated roof leaves a gap, fix pivot/boundary metadata and mesh, not world scale. If removing a wall exposes unfinished backs, model the required back state. Deliver interface/rotation sheet and assembly examples; structural support, placement rules and save behavior need actual runtime implementation.

## Mounted and vehicle exploration

Use the largest supplied rider/vehicle and actual camera offset, turn/height envelope and travel scale. Re-stage the representative route with those proxies: gates, overhangs, landmark sight and surface-detail frequency differ from pedestrian inspection. Place anticipation cues before sharp visual transitions and inspect near-ground as well as elevated camera views.

If a destination reads only when walking, enlarge its primary silhouette or change approach framing; do not claim motion blur proves high-speed readability. If branch/bridge decoration cuts the supplied envelope, relocate it rather than shrinking the mount. Deliver pedestrian-versus-mounted sections and route contact sheet, with speed/camera assumptions. Vehicle handling, traversal and collision response remain untested.

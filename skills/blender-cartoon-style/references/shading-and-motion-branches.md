# Cartoon construction alternatives

Preserve the approved character/prop identity and source. Select soft volume, graphic shading or rubber-hose construction; these systems can combine intentionally but should not be assumed identical. A bright palette alone cannot turn realistic geometry into the selected cartoon language.

## Soft volumetric cartoon

Block torso/head/limbs or prop masses with broad convex regions and deliberate transitions. Test overlap and negative space in a black silhouette. Use coherent bevel/curvature scale so large rounded forms do not carry tiny realistic edge clutter. Separate skin/fabric/glazed/metal response where the design needs it rather than assigning one universal rubber material.

Light with a broad key and controlled fill to reveal turning form. Inspect eye sockets, mouth/spout openings and contact regions in a neutral view before bloom. If the character looks inflated, restore plane changes and structural landmarks; if it looks realistic with saturated paint, alter mass ratios before material tweaking. Accept silhouette, color view, alternate angle and the requested pose at intended size.

## Graphic cartoon

Establish large local-color regions and a small number of intentional shadow/highlight shapes. Choose a renderer-supported band or painted-value method. Keep outer silhouette, internal facial marks and optional line weight separate. EEVEE Shader to RGB and Cycles Toon BSDF are different implementations; do not assume the same graph works in both.

Test a sphere and an actual face/prop under two key angles, then compare cast shadow behavior. If a view-dependent rim is doing all the shaping, restore light-driven or authored value structure. If bands break into noise, simplify normal/detail input and thresholding. Review line-only when used, band/value crop and final-size image. Engine delivery requires a supported tested shader rather than automatic node transfer.

## Rubber-hose or bendable construction

Choose smooth flexible limbs with an explicit range and contact requirements. Build a centerline/curve or suitable evenly distributed deformation topology; do not merely remove elbows from a realistic rig and assume it will bend. Preserve attachment continuity at shoulders, wrists and footwear. Use controls or a deformer suited to the proposed arc, keeping an untouched neutral model.

Block a neutral, extended and strongly curved pose before detailed gloves or face. Inspect limb radius along the bend, socket shape and overlap with torso. Squash/stretch may preserve approximate volume or deliberately change it according to the reference; state which. A bend that collapses into a ribbon needs topology/control/radius repair, not heavier shading. A planted shoe needs stable contact across the action.

Use simple materials and light so deformation is visible. Inspect front/side/three-quarter poses, then watch a short transition between them if animation is requested. A set of static curve shapes is a pose study, not proof of a working animation rig. Deliver source, deformer/control mapping, tested ranges and actual reviewed evidence.

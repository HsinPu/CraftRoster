# Expressive shape production

## Acting premise

Identify personality/action and approved reference. Shape associations depend on context; no primitive carries universal moral meaning. Choose a primary mass and one exaggerated feature. Decide between soft volume, graphic bands and rubber-hose construction.

## Build and test

For a grumpy teapot begin with a squat body, smaller lid and counterbalancing spout/handle. Compare a black silhouette at final size before decoration. Push spout angle to imply attitude while preserving a pour path if required. For a character separate torso, head and limbs in pose before facial detail.

Use broad color regions and focal accents. A soft look can start with roughness 0.45–0.7 and broad area lighting; a graphic look needs designed shadow regions instead. Neither requires outlines. Begin with a broad key and weak fill so cheek/spout volume reads without tiny highlights. Keep texture frequency subordinate to form. For animation preserve a neutral model and test an exaggerated pose. Squash/stretch must retain intended volume and avoid crushed sockets; a material pass cannot prove a usable rig.

## Repair and judge

Photorealistic geometry with bright colors needs changed mass relationships. Noisy contours need small protrusions removed before adding line weight. Compare an unexpectedly aggressive silhouette with the actual reference rather than banning every sharp corner.

Require silhouette, final colored view, alternate three-quarter and a pose/expression image when relevant. Accept identity at small size, coherent exaggeration and shading that supports the acting premise. Preserve the requested palette, soft shading or outline-free design.

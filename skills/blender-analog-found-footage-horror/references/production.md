# Recovered-footage construction

## Choose the device

Separate the fictional recording source from the viewing copy: a lobby camera, shoulder camcorder and digitized broadcast need different shake, lens and tape wear. Pick one event the camera accidentally witnesses. Damage must not become a way to conceal missing staging.

## Production recipe

Build an ordinary room with one suspicious sightline: a doorway at frame edge or a chair facing away. A CCTV study can begin with a high corner mount and 4:3 crop; a camcorder study can begin near 35 mm equivalent at shoulder height. Preserve a clean camera. For handheld, animate a duplicate with slow aim drift and a deliberate corrective pan; random noise on every axis reads as floating machinery. For fixed surveillance, keep the camera fixed.

Use matte walls, dull molded plastic and restrained screen emission. Motivate light with fixtures or a camera-aligned flashlight. Render a clean plate first. In compositing separate luminance softness from chroma displacement. Test 1–2 output pixels of color offset and a short localized tracking disturbance before increasing either. Optional timestamp placement depends on the proposed signal chain: original camera overlay and later editorial caption are different. Never invent real documentary provenance.

## Repair and acceptance

If damage erases the doorway clue, bypass effects, repair blocking, then reduce artifact duration/opacity. If the shot feels like a drone, anchor the operator and motivate pans. Review clean and processed images at delivery size and a contact sheet spanning dropouts and the reveal. Accept when the clue survives, the device premise is consistent and artifacts have hierarchy. View the actual clip before judging animated camera motion; stills are insufficient.

# Anatomical transformation

## Set the boundary

A sealed prosthetic fusion can satisfy this style without exposed tissue. Record subject and intensity. Choose an anatomical baseline and one transformation rule: two fingers sharing a knuckle, or skin becoming ceramic. Keep landmarks that reveal what changed. Wet surfaces, wounds and extra mouths are separate choices.

## Form and surface recipe

Block unchanged anatomy first and duplicate it for the mutation study. For an arm-to-machine transition establish shoulder, elbow and wrist axes before adding a secondary joint or twisted tendon corridor. Distribute asymmetry along the transformation rather than attaching disconnected spikes. Stay with a coarse sculpt/control cage until the silhouette communicates the idea.

Separate skin, transition and foreign material with broad masks. Test dry skin roughness around 0.45–0.65 and a limited damp transition near 0.2–0.35 only when moisture is approved. Metallic remains zero for tissue and nonmetal coatings. Subsurface should soften thin regions without turning the whole body luminous; compare with it disabled before pore detail. A directional side key and weak broad fill expose swelling and material boundaries. Pair a close study with a wider view retaining recognizable anatomy.

## Diagnose and judge

Random kitbashing needs fewer mutations and a continuous anatomical cause. A rubber-toy read needs region-specific gloss and thickness, not more noise. Do not hide interpenetrations in black shadows.

Require neutral clay, a material close-up, an intended final view and unchanged/mutated comparison. Accept when preserved landmarks are coherent, transitions deliberate and intensity approved. If rigged, inspect a relevant bent pose; a resting sculpt cannot establish deformation.

# Visual quality and correction loop

The target is the user's intended asset, viewed in its intended context. "More detailed", "more realistic" and a higher numeric score are not universal improvements.

## Establish the visual target

Use supplied references and established art direction. For an open creation request, choose a coherent provisional direction from the brief and expose it through an early blockout or look-development image; offer alternatives only when they would change a consequential unresolved choice. Existing authorization is sufficient to refine within that direction. Do not stop for confirmation after every routine phase.

Record a few observable goals: recognizable silhouette at gameplay size, plausible relative proportions, readable focal hierarchy, distinct material responses, deliberate palette/value relationships, stable style, and lighting/composition suited to the use. The project determines which goals matter and their tradeoffs.

## Evidence set

Capture a representative view plus side/back or detail views where they expose risks. A typical set includes:

- Neutral or clay lighting to expose form and unwanted intersections.
- Wireframe or a topology diagnostic when geometry is in scope.
- Material/lighting presentation that reflects the intended art direction.
- The actual game camera and screen size, including the relevant motion/pose for animated assets.
- Before/after views using the same comparison settings; keep a separate image for intentional presentation changes.

Not every asset needs every image. The evidence must reveal the changed or risky properties. Never hide poor form, missing backs, clipping or texture defects with darkness, bloom, depth of field, cropping or a single flattering angle. Conversely, a neutral diagnostic image is not a fair substitute for evaluating a deliberately stylized presentation.

## Review and repair

1. Inspect the images, their source revision and the brief. If images are missing, cannot be opened or belong to an older revision, visual status is **unverified**. Structural metrics alone cannot complete this step.
2. Give findings tied to an image/view and visible feature. State the deviation, its effect at the intended camera, proposed smallest correction and recheck evidence. Separate blocking defects from optional taste suggestions.
3. Work from silhouette/proportion and composition toward material readability, light/value/color hierarchy and small detail. Do not add detail to compensate for a wrong primary form.
4. The authorized maker implements corrections; a read-only reviewer returns diagnosis without scene edits, rendering side effects or persisted reports. The maker may serialize that review accurately into the delivery record, retaining reviewer identity and context.
5. Save the corrected source and regenerate affected evidence. Compare like-for-like views and check that fixes did not damage another angle, deformation, budget or export.

Use an independent available reviewer for the final visual pass when practical. Give the reviewer the brief, actual final images/source identity and requested target, without coaching it to approve the maker's choices. If only self-review is possible, label it honestly.

Stop when the agreed requirements are met and there are no unresolved blocking findings; disclose remaining optional polish. If iterations stop improving, isolate the limiting factor (reference ambiguity, inadequate geometry, unavailable renderer or conflicting goals) and report it. Do not loop indefinitely or declare success to satisfy a fixed iteration quota.

## Delivery result

Report technical, visual and engine outcomes separately with evidence and limitations. Quantitative image comparisons can reveal drift but cannot judge overall beauty, physical scale after alignment, or reference fidelity for unseen surfaces. A file/hash checker validates evidence consistency, not the honesty or artistic quality of its report. A user can accept a subjective tradeoff; preserve the recorded preference and do not turn it into a global style rule.

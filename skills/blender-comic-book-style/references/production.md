# Ink and color separation

## Choose print language

Set panel dimensions, color intention and line reference. A brush-ink graphic novel differs from a bright superhero panel. Halftone is optional. Pose large overlapping masses with a clear action direction and leave space for supplied captions. Block paper/light, local color and black anchors before fine detail.

## Produce plates

Choose silhouette, structural crease and secondary-detail edges before generating lines. Render an independent ink pass with a supported line method or authored curves. A hull supplies outer contours but misses many intersections; a camera line pass or authored marks fill that gap. Judge contour weight at final pixels, typically keeping outer edges stronger than facial marks when the reference supports it. Light color to reinforce the ink rather than adding competing soft gradients.

For halftone drive dot coverage from a controlled value mask. Start with 4–8 output-pixel pitch in a digital test and inspect at 100 percent; printed work requires the actual print resolution/process. Restrict tone to selected midtones so lettering and expressions stay clear. Keep an untoned color plate.

## Diagnose and accept

Moiré calls for pitch/angle/output-scale changes rather than blanket blur. Mechanical clutter needs edge filtering or manual emphasis. Merged black masses need pose and negative-space separation.

Require ink-only, color-only and combined panels plus a final-size tone crop. Accept readable action, intentional line hierarchy, stable tone and preserved editorial space. A still panel does not validate animated lines or press reproduction. Preserve source plates for changes in panel placement and output resolution.

# Richer ramps, deliberate clusters

## Set the reference target

Choose an evocative 16-bit look or a specific system/output constraint. SNES and Mega Drive do not have identical palettes, resolutions or display behavior. A generic 16-bit brief does not establish an exact color limit. Record sprite size, output resolution, palette roles and integer enlargement.

## Production recipe

Block the subject to read at its base raster size, such as a 48- or 64-pixel character study. Give major materials distinct hue-shifting ramps: a shadow, middle and light region can start the experiment, with extra colors only where they improve form. Avoid continuous modern gradients by remapping controlled lighting to the selected colors or quantizing a clean render deliberately. Texture detail must remain in clusters rather than isolated noisy pixels.

Use a stable camera and a simple key matching the intended sprite set. Render base-size frames and compare neighboring assets under the same light and pixel scale. Apply ordered or hand-authored dithering only in transitions that benefit from it; large uniform dither across faces and outlines weakens identity. Inspect at 1x and an integer nearest-enlarged preview. If palette cycling is requested, reserve indexed color roles and test the actual sequence; a static Blender color texture does not implement engine palette animation.

## Repair and acceptance

An 8-bit-looking result may need more expressive ramps and hue variation, not merely higher resolution. A modern airbrushed result needs selected color grouping. Noisy dither needs fewer affected regions and larger deliberate clusters.

Require base raster, enlarged preview, palette/ramp swatches and a neighboring-asset comparison. Animated delivery needs a contact sheet plus viewed clip. Accept rich but disciplined small-scale form, consistent lighting/pixel scale and a clear stated authenticity level. Do not call arbitrary posterization hardware-accurate.

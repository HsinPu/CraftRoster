# An inviting place

## Define comfort

Comfort can mean a cool rainy nook, a sunny kitchen or a snowy workshop. Keep season and palette; orange lighting is optional. Choose a focal activity such as reading beside a seat. Establish where to enter and rest before adding clutter.

## Construct and light

Block a seat, task surface and nearby light as a readable group. Round plausible contact edges and leave circulation visible. Build cushions from broad compressed forms, wood with directional grain and ceramics with broad highlights. Cloth roughness near 0.75 and glazed ceramic near 0.25 can serve as a contrast experiment, then tune against references rather than applying one matte material everywhere.

Use a broad window or shaded practical for a gradual focal pool. Test fill contributing roughly a quarter to a half of the key at the subject, then judge actual shadow readability. A cool rainy window and warmer lamp can coexist; a fully warm room needs value separation instead. Keep the camera near the activity rather than looming over tiny occupants. Limit halation until mug rims and book edges remain clear.

## Repair and acceptance

Hazardous clutter needs cleared circulation and grouping by use. Plastic-looking furniture needs separate cloth, wood and ceramic responses. A flat scene needs a stronger light hierarchy before higher saturation.

Inspect the final activity view, a grayscale thumbnail and a wider layout image showing access. Accept when the resting place is evident, shadows retain information, surfaces suggest touch/use and decoration supports the activity. Compare the same before/after camera for existing-scene polish so improvement does not replace identity.

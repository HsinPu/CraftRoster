# Historical and Period World Kits: production playbook

## Brief, scale and evidence

Name place, date range, social context and fidelity goal. Distinguish documented reconstruction, inference, stylization and alternate history. Broad labels cannot establish one culture or costume; preserve selected references and record uncertainty.

## Asset kit and construction

Dated architectural bay, doorway/roof junction, everyday tool, container, clothing/equipment proxy and optional transport element. Maintain a feature-to-source evidence ledger.

Model wall core/finish, joinery, fasteners and repairs according to referenced methods. Avoid universal grime/gold. Mark inferred thickness and concealed mechanisms; keep speculative alternatives as named variants.

## Bounded layout recipe

These dimensions are a starting study, not established game rules.

1. Choose one bounded frontage/interior and establish person/tool scale from dated sources.
2. Assign each major feature an evidence status and date/place range; resolve incompatible roofs, garments and weapons before assembly.
3. Build a construction junction and daily prop, then repeat the bay; museum wear is not automatically original use condition.
4. Capture neutral period-check views and annotated exceptions; alternate-history technology needs explicit mounting/service changes.

## Diagnostic images and acceptance

Construction cutaway, scale/material sheet and dated ledger beside matching renders. Inspect human-eye silhouettes. Technical validity and plausibility cannot prove authenticity when evidence is absent.

## Specific failures and repairs

Mixed centuries: replace unsupported parts or explicitly label fiction. Modern optics/fasteners in early kit: verify or remove. Unsourced cultural motif: retain neutral form until research supports it; do not quietly widen the date range.

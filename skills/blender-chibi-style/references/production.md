# Compact character recipe

## Proportion contract

Measure total height in head units. A 2.5-head study places the head near 40 percent of height, but supplied sheets override this. Large eyes are optional. Identify costume features that must survive compression.

## Construct the character

Block head, torso and pelvis as distinct simple masses. Redesign limb volumes and joint spacing instead of uniformly shrinking realistic anatomy. Leave shoulder/neck clearance for the head to turn. Choose mitten hands or simplified fingers from the brief and separate hands from the torso silhouette. Test boots, sleeves and hat at final display size before detailing accessories.

Place facial features on a coherent curved surface and inspect front and three-quarter views. Group hair into readable locks. Mostly matte skin with controlled eye reflection can prioritize expression: try skin roughness near 0.55 and eyes near 0.15 as a comparison, then tune to reference. A broad key and gentle fill prevent deep miniature eye-socket shadows. Use near-face camera height and moderate perspective to avoid accidental top-heavy distortion.

## Repair and acceptance

A realistic torso below a giant head needs whole-body mass redesign. Merged limb silhouettes need spacing/pose correction. Tiny accessories need grouping or enlargement according to identity, not indiscriminate deletion. Do not hide joint collisions behind the head.

Require front, side, three-quarter and small silhouette images. If articulation is requested include a head turn or raised-arm/crouch pose. Accept deliberate head-unit ratios, costume recognition, readable expression and tested clearance. A static mesh does not establish a working rig.

# Polygon economy as design

## Set the target

Choose modern faceted, smooth low-count, early-console-inspired or another explicit reference. Low polygon count does not inherently require horror, fog, tiny textures or flat shading. Record evaluated triangle limit, display distance and silhouette features that must survive. Budget depends on actual use rather than a universal asset table.

## Modeling study

Block a prop from primary volumes, then inspect silhouette at final screen coverage. For a small hand tool test roughly 150–400 triangles, adding segments only where visible curvature changes identity. Count evaluated triangles after modifiers and triangulation, not editable face count. Retain a high-level source if reduction is destructive. Allocate more edges to silhouette bends and deforming joints than hidden planar interiors.

Use a compact palette or atlas when compatible with the brief. A 128–256-pixel prop test can demonstrate texture sufficiency, but do not force small maps onto a large hero close-up. Flat normals emphasize facets; selectively smooth normals preserve low-count rounded forms. Compare both under broad grazing light. Use a bevel only when its cost visibly supports the chosen style. Modern low-poly can use restrained PBR; authentic retro constraints are separate choices.

## Repair and acceptance

A decimated blob needs feature-aware topology reconstruction, not more global decimation. Tiny bevels that consume budget but vanish in the final view should be removed or baked where appropriate. Facets that look accidental need deliberate plane grouping or normal correction. Do not introduce z-fighting as an aesthetic shortcut.

Require silhouette, wireframe/evaluated-count report, neutral shaded view and intended-camera image. Accept identity at target size, within-budget evaluated geometry and a coherent selected shading language. Affine warping and screen-space vertex jitter require an explicit implementation; Blender low-count geometry alone does not reproduce console rendering.

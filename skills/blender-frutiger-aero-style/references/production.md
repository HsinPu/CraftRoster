# Optimistic nature and interface objects

## Choose the composition

Identify the reference period and purpose: glossy device vignette, nature-tech still life or spacious promotional environment. Choose one natural anchor and one technological form. Aqua, blue and fresh green are useful reference cues, but preserve supplied branding and avoid copying logos or interface assets.

## Construct the study

Build a rounded white device beside one grass or water element, framed by a glass sphere or translucent ribbon. Give glass actual closed thickness; avoid intersecting nested transparent surfaces until the simple version renders cleanly. Shape the device with broad bevels that generate continuous highlights rather than adding chrome edges everywhere.

Test dielectric white casing around roughness 0.2–0.35 and a clearer orb around 0.05–0.15. Keep metallic zero for plastic/glass; transmission and IOR describe transparency, not a metallic slider. A glass IOR near 1.45 is a starting test, subordinate to the chosen material. Use large reflected light cards or area sources so gloss describes curvature. Add a bright sky or soft studio backdrop and retain readable whites below clipping. Small droplets need contact shadows/refraction, not floating opaque beads.

## Repair and acceptance

If it becomes synthwave, remove dark neon grids and return to clean nature-tech relationships. If transparency becomes a black blob, check thickness, normals, overlap and renderer refraction support before raising emission. If white objects lose form, reshape broad reflections and exposure.

Require final image, neutral material crop and a reflection/transmission diagnostic view. Accept buoyant clean composition, distinct plastic/glass/water responses, intact highlight shape and recognizable natural context. A Blender promotional render does not establish a functioning skeuomorphic UI or portable real-time glass.

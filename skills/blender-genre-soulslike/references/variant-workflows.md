# Monumental, return-loop and threshold workflows

Use original project vocabulary and supplied actor/camera dimensions; do not copy a franchise asset or assume rest points must be bonfires. Melancholy, vulnerability and challenge are art goals rather than verified gameplay outcomes.

## Monumental inhabited ruins

Block one large structural bay around human-scale steps, handles, door inserts and usable routes. Separate ceremonial silhouette from actual opening/contact dimensions. Build an intact master, then damaged/abandoned or repaired derivative with coherent support and exposure history. A 24 m tall study should still explain how a person enters and where floors/landings lie.

Use broad stone/metal/organic value groups and restrained practicals, with a bright neutral cutaway. If the actor disappears into grey stone, repair large-value/light separation before adding an outline or fog. If doors look usable at impossible scale, add a smaller entrance or identify ceremonial framing. Deliver human/monument sections, intact/derived pair and eye/hero views; structural stability and encounter challenge are not established.

## Vertical loops and remembered shortcuts

Build lower arrival, upper return and a persistent landmark visible from both levels. Use supplied stairs/elevator/ladders as shell/proxy contracts; measure matching landings and opening heights. Stage the shortcut so its relation to the original arrival can be understood visually without a label. Keep intermediate shaft/interior/back sides modeled where cameras can see them.

Test entry, upper reveal and reverse-return cameras with atmosphere disabled. If an elevator opening floats above its landing, repair geometry/state rather than inventing a lift behavior. If the landmark is lost in the upper view, alter its outline or occluding masses. Deliver vertical section, landing measurements and paired remembered-view images. This cannot prove that a lift operates, a shortcut unlocks or progression is connected in the game.

## Rest landmark and safe-space contrast

Use supplied safe/rest vocabulary; build a recognizable object/place and active/inactive states with stable interaction anchor. Surround it with a quieter composition, some human-scale use trace and distinct material/light hierarchy. Safe space can be daylight, shelter or a practical light pool; it need not be warm orange or a fire.

Inspect return approach and minimum screen size in grayscale/emission-off. If the marker is indistinguishable from decoration, strengthen physical frame/negative space. If every practical uses the same safe-state cue, differentiate ordinary fixtures. Deliver state/anchor sheet and arrival/return views. Art cues do not establish saving, healing or actual enemy exclusion.

## Mist thresholds and encounter readability

Build an original threshold frame with explicit opening, mist layer/volume and active/inactive state. Keep the frame recognizable when mist is dense and model the route beyond sufficiently for requested views. Test the largest supplied actor/weapon silhouette at camera edges and behind local obstructions, with effects off/on.

If fog hides both frame and actor, reduce coverage or revise lighting/mass separation rather than declare ambiguity intentional. If floor cracks resemble danger cues, quiet the pattern. Deliver threshold state pairs, actor-edge contact sheet and obstruction samples. Difficulty never justifies falsely claiming invisible hazards or broken camera art passed; combat fairness and fog-gate behavior need actual runtime/playtest evidence.

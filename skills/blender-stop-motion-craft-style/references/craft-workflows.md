# Craft construction and stop-motion branches

Choose a material family and apparent miniature scale before modeling. Build the assembly the audience should believe exists. Handmade irregularity should be stable and purposeful; random vertex noise is not craftsmanship. Preserve the main recipe's source, camera and image-review requirements.

## Clay puppet

Block a head/body and two limbs as compressed rounded masses around a simple proposed support structure. Decide which joints are concealed within clay and which seams are visible. Keep a neutral original and a posed duplicate. At a bent elbow or squeezed cheek, sculpt broad compression before fingerprint detail. If an armature is requested, place joints to preserve the clay silhouette and test bending rather than assuming a continuous mesh is sufficient.

Create separate masks for base clay, handled/smoothed patches and small tool seams. Fingerprints should follow plausible handling directions and have a scale related to the puppet, with many regions left quiet. Use restrained bump/normal and broad roughness variation; compare a clean patch to a handled patch under the same softbox. Surface color can vary subtly within the chosen clay, but painted features should remain distinct layers when the construction implies paint.

Place the miniature on a physical-looking set with visible contact and broad source reflections. Inspect the face with flat light and a grazing key: gravel-like relief needs lower amplitude/larger scale, while plastic gloss needs material separation or roughness correction rather than more fingerprints. Accept recognizable compressed form, credible contact and handling marks that remain subordinate at final size. Rendered fingerprints do not prove real clay or a functional physical armature.

## Folded paper and card

Design a net or panel diagram: front/back faces, fold lines, tabs and joining edges. Build from planar sections with slight real thickness, crisp folds and visible cut edges. Rounded clay-like cheeks contradict a folded-card construction unless made from documented curved/bent sheets. Use folds, scored hinges and overlapping tabs to explain the volume, especially at the reverse side.

Assign front printing, back stock and cut-edge materials separately when visible. Keep fibers much smaller and lower contrast than folds. A crease can use geometry, a shaded fold line or both, but large crumple bump across every panel changes the intended object. For curved paper, use a smooth controlled bend without losing sheet thickness or introducing self-intersections.

Light obliquely enough for tabs and overlaps to cast small contact shadows. Capture front, reverse, assembly-exploded and final miniature views. If it reads as concrete, reduce grain and restore flat panels; if it reads as perfect CAD plastic, add plausible cut/fold variation and stock edge response. If tabs pass through adjacent sheets, repair the net/assembly rather than hiding the reverse. Acceptance requires readable paper construction from the requested views, coherent thickness and retained printed graphics.

## Felt, sewn cloth and plush

Start with panel seams and stuffing masses. A plush form bulges between seams and compresses at contacts; a thin felt cutout can remain sheet-like. Model stitches at hero distance or use a correctly scaled normal/texture for farther work. Seams should follow how panels could join, not random decorative loops through the face.

Establish a matte base and restrained grazing fiber response. Short fibers, fuzz cards or curves are optional representations chosen by camera and budget; a coarse normal texture is not equivalent to long fur. Keep eyes/buttons/embroidered features physically distinct. Inspect fiber length against seam size, head size and the final pixel footprint before adding density.

Use broad light for shape and one grazing diagnostic for fibers. Overgrown fuzz hides the seam and puppet silhouette: reduce length/density selectively. Gravel-like felt needs lower bump amplitude and finer frequency. A deflated-toy read needs stuffing/pose/contact correction instead of roughness. Deliver a seam diagram, material crop and neutral/final images; rigged motion needs a bent pose and viewed clip if claimed.

## Carved wood and articulated puppet

Lay out head/torso/limb pieces, hinge or dowel axes, visible sockets and optional string attachments. Preserve articulation gaps rather than sculpting all limbs into one seamless organic body. Carved planes and grain direction should agree with the chosen piece orientation; end grain belongs at cut ends, not uniformly on every face.

Use subtle tool facets and separate painted versus exposed wood response. Place pivot empties or bones at actual joints and test a small range for hinge penetration. Marionette strings, if requested, need anchored endpoints and a supported path; visible strings alone do not establish a simulated mechanism.

A plastic-toy read can need more useful facets and grain orientation, not universal scratches. Joints intersecting their housings need clearance/pivot repair. Capture joint close-ups at neutral/extreme pose and a final miniature view. Accept the stated wood assembly and pose, while leaving mechanical or dynamic claims unverified unless executed.

## Stepped animation and photographed miniature behavior

Choose shot FPS and exposure separately from pose sampling. Holding each pose for two frames at 24 FPS is a 12-pose-per-second study, not a universal stop-motion rule. Block key acting poses, anticipation, contact and recovery before stepping. Use Constant keys or explicit repeated samples as appropriate; inspect all animated channels, including eyes and material changes, so one smooth channel does not accidentally undermine the chosen hold pattern.

Keep a planted foot or hand stable across held poses. Design spacing between poses instead of adding random jitter. Camera motion may remain smooth or be stepped deliberately, and motion blur may be absent or part of the reference; choose rather than automatically doubling the pose spacing. A changed light or exposure between frames is flicker, not automatically a craft cue.

Review frame contact sheets and actual playback, including first/last and held contact frames. Repair slipping contacts in geometry/poses, not with blur. Deliver source actions, hold pattern, FPS/range, lighting/camera settings and the viewed preview. A still miniature render proves neither stop-motion acting nor temporal stability.

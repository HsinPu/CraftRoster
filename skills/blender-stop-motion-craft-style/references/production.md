# Material honesty and performance

## Select the craft

Choose clay, folded paper, felt/plush or wood before modeling. Each needs different joins and deformation. Handmade does not mean random damage. Record apparent physical scale and whether stepped animation is requested; a still-image brief does not require adding a rig.

## Construction recipes

For clay, use rounded compressed forms with selective seam/fingerprint marks tied to handling. Start mostly matte near roughness 0.65–0.85 and keep fingerprint scale subtle relative to the puppet. For paper, construct planar folds with slight thickness, tabs and contact shadows; paper grain must not resemble concrete bump. Felt needs soft stuffing silhouette, seams and short fine fibers, while wood needs carved planes, grain direction and hinges or dowels. Keep materials separate instead of applying the same procedural noise to all four.

Light like a practical miniature set with a broad source and controlled shadow falloff. Camera depth of field can signal small scale, but keep focal features sharp. Leave tiny asymmetries in edge or assembly only when they remain plausible and stable. Avoid perfectly repeated fingerprints.

For motion test a stepped action on a duplicate: at 24 fps, holding poses for two frames gives a 12-pose-per-second experiment. Use constant interpolation or explicit holds as appropriate, while separating camera motion and motion blur choices. Stepped frames alone do not create convincing stop-motion acting; check spacing, anticipation and contact.

## Repairs and acceptance

Plastic clay needs roughness/handling cues. Crumpled paper needs fold structure and restrained grain. Random jitter needs intentional held poses and contact repair.

Require assembly/material crops, final miniature view and a neutral construction image. Animated delivery also needs viewed playback and contact-sheet timing. Accept recognizable craft, plausible scale, stable imperfections and deliberate performance; no horror mutation is implied by puppet materials.

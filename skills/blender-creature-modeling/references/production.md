# Creature structure and production

## Locomotion families

For quadrupeds, distinguish the true knee/stifle from the hock (ankle), and the forelimb elbow from the wrist. Label joint guides so a visually backward-looking lower-leg bend does not become a reversed knee. Compare an appropriate species reference for scapular movement, ribcage, pelvis and foot contact. Species differ: universal claims such as a fixed 60% mass distribution or sideways galloping spine are unreliable. Use gait references to establish the design's actual compression and extension.

Winged designs need a chosen structural model. Bird wings emphasize modified arm/hand segments and feather layers; bat wings use elongated digits supporting a membrane. A six-limbed dragon is a deliberate fantasy departure and needs clear wing/leg attachments. Place the wing root, chest support and folded clearance before adding scales. Wingspan-to-body ratios vary widely; choose from the approved design rather than treating a 2–5x guide as a flight guarantee. Check both spanwise and chordwise membrane deformation with actual poses.

Aquatic forms read through body taper, fin placements and propulsion rhythm. Serpentine creatures need consistent sectional transitions and bending space along the spine. Arthropods need distinct rigid segments and joint clearance. For hybrids, separate biological tissue, armor and mechanical pivots: rigid parts should not inherit soft deformation accidentally.

## Form hierarchy and topology

Build skull, ribcage, pelvis and limb masses first. Secondary surfaces should explain muscle origins/insertions, bony landmarks and fat transitions; invented scars should not conceal unresolved anatomy. Tertiary folds follow compression and stretch directions. Scales and pores usually belong in a dense source and baked maps when real-time budgets cannot sustain them.

Density is highest where outline, face and moving joints need it. Preserve enough loops near shoulders/hips for gait and attack poses; reduce hidden regions only after all camera and movement requirements are known. Membrane grids must support the actual folding pattern; no one edge direction is sufficient for every wing. Keep tails and horns readable from the gameplay view. A boss close-up and a background swarm creature have different surface budgets.

## Worked winged quadruped

For a 3 m-long grounded creature with a 6 m approved wingspan, mark shoulder, hip, stifle, hock, paw and wing finger guides. Build chest and pelvis volumes and place a human scale guide outside export. Test a walking support pose and a folded-wing silhouette with simple masses. Ensure folded wing tips clear the rear leg and the head can reach the attack pose without shoulder penetration. Add wing membrane as a separate surface with enough crosswise rows to hold folds. Keep plated armor independently addressable.

Once these views read correctly, sculpt shoulder/haunch transitions and membrane folds on a separate dense copy. Build a lower mesh around joint motion and projected silhouette, then test full extension, crouch and wing fold with rough deformers or a permitted rig. Do not interpret a visually plausible pose as aerodynamic verification.

## Failure and acceptance

Floating limb attachments require mass and joint relocation, not extra wrinkles. A membrane tearing on fold requires topology/clearance and rig review. Uniform high-frequency noise can destroy a creature's readable scale; reduce micro-detail and inspect at the actual camera size. Rigid plates bending with flesh need an object/weight boundary correction.

Accept technical structure only after finite geometry, boundaries, modifiers, topology budgets and part ownership are checked. Accept appearance only from current front/side/three-quarter and gameplay images, plus the required poses. Retain the sculpt, lower mesh and pose evidence separately. Label an anatomical design choice as artistic plausibility; neither measurements nor a successful rig build establish biological or flight realism.

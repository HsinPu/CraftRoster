# Racing and sports production branches

## Racing approach, bend and exit

Use supplied vehicle dimensions, driver camera/FOV, road section, banking and speed samples. Build centerline and full cross-section—surface, crown/bank, curb, barrier and runoff—before dressing. Adjacent modules need matching position, tangent and cross-section orientation; centerline continuity alone does not remove a banking seam. Place turn-in/apex/exit cameras and a recognizable anticipatory landmark before the bend.

Keep critical edge/curb values distinct from sponsors/decals. Test sharp frames first, then a timed camera traversal if authorized; blur alone cannot prove readability at speed. If a curb looks like road, revise profile/value and material scale. If a barrier crosses runoff, repair placement and connections. Deliver centerline/section data, approach sequence and seam close-ups; no handling, braking or track safety claim from Blender geometry.

## Field sports

Obtain actual sport/ruleset/date dimensions when regulation fidelity is requested; otherwise label fictional dimensions. Build playing surface, boundaries/scoring geometry, athlete proxy, runoff/access and one stand bay. Keep line markings independent of sponsor graphics and preserve their exact planar relation to the surface. Grass geometry should not obscure lines or create a false ball/contact height.

Test athlete-eye, near sideline and far broadcast cameras. Use broad field values and team separation without recoloring critical markings. If distant goals/lines vanish, correct geometry/width/contrast within the actual rules rather than inventing dimensions. Deliver dimension/source ledger and field/corner/access sections. Rendered line placement does not prove rule compliance, ball physics or athlete navigation.

## Court sports

Select supplied court geometry, equipment and camera contract. Model surface thickness/contact, boundary lines, net/hoop/equivalent structure and supporting hardware, including backs visible to broadcast/replay cameras. Use real measured or explicitly proposed athlete scale for reach/clearance. Separate floor finish/reflection from permanent line graphics.

Inspect low athlete, end-court and elevated broadcast views with a neutral roughness diagnostic. If glossy flooring erases markings, reduce inappropriate reflection or improve physical lighting without changing line positions. If support hardware blocks required views, revise owned staging or provide the approved alternative. Deliver equipment pivot/attachment and dimensional sheet, near/far images and source uncertainties; no ball/contact behavior or official certification claim.

## Arena and vehicle sports

Use actual participant vehicle/body, arena boundary and scoring vocabulary rather than transplanting racing geometry. Build a representative goal/corner/boundary junction with explicit bank/curve and player-camera envelope. Scoring structures need active/inactive states with stable physical opening dimensions; sponsor lights must remain distinguishable from target cues.

Inspect participant-eye and the widest requested spectator/replay view, including simultaneous proxy overlap. If a decorative arch looks like a scoring opening, change its silhouette or placement. If banking seams change visible contact height, reconcile complete sections. Deliver boundary/goal states and camera/section sheet; scoring, vehicle handling and collision response are runtime questions.

## Stadium stands and background variants

Build a repeated seating/access bay with aisle, railing and support, then join straight and corner variants before arraying. Keep real athlete/seat references distinct from a stylized crowd texture. Make near/far versions preserving primary stand silhouette; distance-appropriate simplification must retain required access silhouettes and important signs.

If repeated bays crack at corners or signs reverse, correct pivot/rotation/UV convention. Inspect far zoom and grazing aisle views, with crowd effects disabled. Deliver bay/connector/LOD screen-size comparison and instance/export membership. Counts alone cannot establish crowd rendering performance, accessibility or venue safety.

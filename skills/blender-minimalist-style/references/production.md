# Deliberate reduction

## Identify what must remain

State the subject, distinguishing feature and compositional intention. A minimal kettle may need only body, spout and handle, but removing the spout would erase identity. Minimalism can be matte or glossy, geometric or organic; do not assume a monochrome white studio. Keep approved brand and palette.

## Reduction recipe

Duplicate the source, then temporarily hide secondary details by collection or modifier. Test the subject as a black silhouette at the final size. Reintroduce only elements that materially restore identity, function or balance. Prefer coherent planes and curves over arbitrary decimation. Use 1–3 material families as an experiment when the brief permits, not a fixed restriction.

Place one dominant mass and a deliberate surrounding gap. Compare centered symmetry with a small offset against the user's composition rather than defaulting to a fashionable layout. Use a single broad source and minimal fill to reveal form, or a hard graphic source if the reference is flat. Start with one consistent roughness per material family, then add a meaningful contrast rather than procedural noise. Keep shadows controlled so they support the negative-space design. Composite lightly; excess grain and glow can undermine reduction.

## Diagnose and accept

Generic shapes need the distinguishing feature restored. Empty but unbalanced composition needs mass/space adjustment, not more decoration. A flat unreadable object needs lighting or value separation. Retain recoverable source detail for later needs rather than deleting unrelated production components.

Require before/after silhouette, final image and small-size value study. Accept recognition, intentional spacing and a reduction whose remaining elements each serve the brief. A lower object count alone does not establish better design or target-runtime performance.

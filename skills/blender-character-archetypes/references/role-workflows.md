# Role kit construction and correction

Use the chosen body workflow first. Each role needs one dominant tool/action, a secondary occupation cue and a working pose; do not rely on hat or hue alone. Existing cultural/historical references take precedence over these fictional kit proposals. Props need named grip, sheath/holster and attachment landmarks.

## Knight

Build armor by articulation zones: torso shell, shoulder, upper/lower limb plates and flexible joints. Fit a shield/weapon to actual hand/grip, then test raised arm, seated and crouched proxies. Distinguish coating, bare edges and cloth without making all armor uniformly silver. If the elbow bridges, split/overlap plates with bend clearance. Deliver armor-layer and grip sheets plus neutral/action silhouettes; a rigid pose study is not a tested deformation rig.

## Samurai

Choose a supplied or researched date/culture and distinguish historical reconstruction from fantasy. Block armor/garment layers and attachment before ornaments, then test seated/kneeling and weapon/sheath clearance with the body. Keep rigid plates and flexible ties separate. If unsupported motifs or mixed equipment appear, replace or label them rather than treating a broad role name as evidence. Neutral side light should reveal layering. Deliver dated feature ledger when fidelity matters and layer/working-pose views.

## Ninja and covert specialist

Use the actual role/context instead of assuming a universal historical black costume. Build mobility-oriented clothing, carry system and one supplied tool with stowed/deployed states. Test crouch, climb/reach and hand access to storage. Separate cloth fold mass from rigid equipment. If the role vanishes without dark color, strengthen loadout/pose and task-specific silhouette. Deliver equipment-state and reach views; concealment, climbing or historical authenticity needs its own evidence.

## Pirate

Establish maritime task and era/fantasy level, then build clothing compatible with that work and a small equipment kit with reachable carry points. Test rope/tool grip and deck stance rather than stacking unrelated skull/weapon motifs. Distinguish salt/water-exposed wear from protected lining. If a coat/weapon intersects the working hand or deck, repair attachment/length. Deliver stowed/working states and period assumptions; sailing/combat behavior is outside the asset study.

## Cowboy and frontier worker

Choose occupation, locality/date and activity such as mounted work or repair. Fit hat/outerwear, boots, tool belt and a role-specific item to the approved body, then check seated/mounted proxy and arm reach. Use cloth/leather/metal groups with handling wear, avoiding dust on every protected surface. If accessories collide with the saddle/seat or obscure hands, repair carry points. Deliver standing/seated/action silhouettes and sourced exceptions when historical fidelity is required.

## Soldier

Obtain year/organization/role and loadout constraints before modeling. Build garment base, protective equipment and carry modules around actual access/weight distribution, with neutral unmarked source if markings are unverified. Test crouch, prone-like proxy and shoulder reach as requested, without claiming tactics or equipment function. If pouches block joints/grip, move local mounts. Deliver dated inventory, attachment sheet and neutral/action views; costume readability does not prove operational suitability.

## Detective

Define setting and investigative task rather than defaulting to a trench coat. Build one evidence/tool interaction and reachable storage, with clothing mass supporting face/hand visibility. Compare relaxed and focused working poses at the story camera. Use restrained material accents on the tool, not glow on every pocket. If the role is only a hat, strengthen the action/loadout and prop state. Deliver seated/standing/tool-close-up views and continuity landmarks for narrative shots.

## Assassin

Use original role fiction and supplied movement; do not let it dictate morality or one cultural costume. Build a compact carry system, task-specific tool and deploy/stow states around joint clearance. Test reach and silhouette from the intended camera, keeping ornamental cloth clear of the hand/action. If a long silhouette element intersects the ground or conceals required motion, adjust its owned attachment/length. Deliver rest/action/stowed states; stealth/detection and weapon behavior remain untested.

## Mercenary

Define service history and equipment procurement so a mixed kit has a reason. Build a maintained core with a few mismatched replacements sharing plausible attachment interfaces. Separate recent repair from old wear and retain an unmarked variant. If every piece has unrelated scale/technology, unify mounts, manufacturing rhythm and primary material groups. Deliver loadout inventory, repair-state close-ups and working-pose clearances; visual history does not establish combat performance.

## Superhero

Design an original dominant silhouette and symbol system around the stated abilities and identity. Build suit/armor/appendages as layers, with a neutral civilian-scale proxy where useful. Test cape/wing/large emblem clearance and hand/face read in a signature pose and relaxed pose. If emission or saturated color alone carries recognition, strengthen large forms. Deliver clean/emission-off and action silhouettes plus attachment states; supernatural capability is fiction, not simulated evidence.

## Villain or antagonist

Start from individual role and story function instead of equating disability, ethnicity or body type with evil. Choose a distinct authority/tool/costume hierarchy and build its actual attachments/use state. Contrast primary masses and one deliberate asymmetry only when the brief supports it. If the result relies on generic spikes/black palette, refine occupation and face/hand hierarchy. Deliver neutral and story-action views with the approved symbol/material system; audience reaction is not measurable from a single render.

## Witch

Use the supplied cultural/fantasy context and actual practice, not compulsory caricature. Build a portable or stationary working kit with containers, tool grips and active/inactive ritual elements. Test hand access, seated/kneeling posture and long clothing clearance. Separate ordinary fabric/wood/metal from any magical material. If every prop glows, reserve emission for the current action and reveal ordinary construction. Deliver working-kit layout, rest/action and magic-off views, preserving original lore and rights.

## Wizard

Define what the character does with magic and how the kit supports it: staff/book/instrument are options, not mandatory. Build grip/opening/pivot states for the chosen focus and clothing layers around arm reach. A book needs thickness, hinge and page block; a staff needs hand/ground clearance. If the focus hides the face or clips the shoulder, repair staging and attachment before changing body scale. Deliver readable thumbnail silhouette, prop-state sheet and neutral/magic comparison; effects and game ability logic are separate handoffs.

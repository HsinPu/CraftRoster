# Morphology and body-family workflows

Archetypes are optional design hypotheses, not biological facts or moral/cultural traits. Preserve individual identity and the supplied body/movement contract. Work from a proportion mannequin; compare equal-height front/side/three-quarter silhouettes before costume. These studies do not prove a finished deformation rig.

## Elf and elongated silhouettes

If elongated proportions are requested, lengthen selected torso/limb rhythms coherently rather than uniformly stretching the whole mesh. Block pelvis, rib cage, shoulder and knee landmarks; compare hand reach, seated height and tool grip at the approved total scale. Keep ear design an authored feature with actual attachment/thickness. Use restrained costume planes/material contrast to support long lines, then light from broad side/front to reveal volume. If the figure reads thin/fragile unintentionally, correct mass at joints and torso instead of hiding it with armor. Deliver proportion ratios, neutral/reach/seated poses and ear close-up; ancestry alone must not dictate elegance or personality.

## Dwarf and compact heavy silhouettes

Design compact mass through torso/pelvis/limb ratios and center of visual weight, retaining believable reach for the character's work. Build a tool, workbench and door proxy at that actual body scale. Separate thick costume/armor from underlying joint space; broad forearms and short upper arms need an explicit elbow bend study. Use broad matte/metal groups rather than dense tiny ornament. If tools collide with torso or knees in a working pose, resize/reposition the owned kit or revise approved proportions, not silently shrink the character. Deliver reach/equipment/seat checks and neutral silhouette lineup.

## Orc and goblin alternatives

Treat these as distinct user-authored populations rather than one compulsory jaw/shoulder stereotype. Build alternative mass distributions: a broad high-torso form and a lighter low-center form only if appropriate to the brief. Establish skull/jaw/neck support and hand/foot contact before teeth, spikes or costume. Keep silhouettes readable in grayscale with material roles that do not encode morality. If the only distinction is green skin, revise proportions and authored tools/posture. If large jaw/teeth prevent supplied expressions, repair oral clearance or adapt the design explicitly. Deliver face profile, grip and neutral/action silhouettes with named fictional assumptions.

## Fairy and small winged bodies

Set absolute scale, not just screen size: a 0.2 m figure and a human-sized winged person need different prop/contact cues. Build wing roots, folded/open envelopes and a perch/handhold at that scale. Separate membranes/veins or feather masses from body support; transparency needs a real material/alpha test under the target renderer. Use a backlight plus a neutral opaque-wing diagnostic. If wings vanish, strengthen structural silhouette before increasing emission; if folded wings intersect a costume, repair attachment/hinge envelope. Deliver open/folded/perched views and alpha-on/off comparisons; flight mechanics remain untested.

## Vampire

Build an individual with a chosen predatory/elegant or other approved contrast, not an automatic cape-and-pale-skin package. Establish head/neck profile, mouth/teeth clearance and costume mass around the face. Use one role-specific prop or contact pose to distinguish the individual. Control skin, cloth and metal roughness under neutral light before dramatic rim. If identity disappears without red eyes, strengthen profile/costume proportions. If fangs intersect lips, repair mouth geometry and expression extremes. Deliver neutral and open-mouth/action studies; supernatural lore is supplied fiction rather than a rig capability.

## Werewolf and hybrid limb design

Choose explicit transition between human and animal anatomy, stance and paw/hand use. Block spine/pelvis, digitigrade or plantigrade legs, shoulder rotation and jaw before fur. Use a ground-contact side view and bent-limb proxy to prevent a backward-bending joint illusion. Fur is a separate silhouette layer with a smooth-body diagnostic. If bulk hides knees/elbows, redistribute masses and groom rather than hoping animation will explain them. Deliver anatomy/stance notes, crouch/reach/contact studies and fur-off/on silhouettes. A hybrid appearance is not a verified locomotion or deformation solution.

## Ghost and spectral body

Create a readable opaque source form, then choose missing lower body, trailing cloth or other approved spectral anatomy. Model trails from a stable torso attachment with controlled taper; preserve separate front/back layers to avoid alpha piles. Test opacity and additive/transparent choices against black, gray and bright backgrounds in the actual renderer. If the face disappears or layers sort incorrectly, simplify overlap and restore solid value anchors before more glow. Deliver opaque diagnostic, transparency variants and short motion preview if trails animate; ordinary exports may need baked cards or custom target shaders.

## Zombie and decay asymmetry

Begin from an intact human source and apply a localized, authored damage/decay history. Separate missing volume, displaced cloth, exposed hard parts and staining rather than adding uniform noise. Preserve enough support at weight-bearing joints and enough face structure for required expressions. Use neutral flesh/cloth/material separation, then intended low light. If asymmetry becomes random silhouette clutter, consolidate to one dominant damage direction. If animation exposes unsupported floating parts, repair attachment or identify deliberate supernatural suspension. Deliver intact/decayed pair and joint/face close-ups; gore intensity and anatomy changes remain within the brief.

## Demon and angel heraldic extremes

Define original cosmology and individual role; wings/horns/halos do not mandate morality or copied religious imagery. Build the core body first, then attachment and folded/open envelopes for appendages. Distinguish rigid horns/armor from deforming membranes/feathers. Use large heraldic masses and limited accent material so the face/hands remain legible. If every feature competes, reduce secondary spikes/feathers. If the silhouette clips ordinary doors, supply an alternate folded state or explicitly mark the architecture mismatch. Deliver front/side, appendage states and minimum-screen-size silhouette; ritual meaning and flight remain authored/untested.

## Mutant and beast

Specify one dominant anatomical transformation and its effect on support, movement and tool use. Model a neutral underlying skeleton/mass proxy, then added limbs/growth with explicit attachment and range. Avoid piling unrelated mutations until joint function becomes unintelligible. Use material transitions to show growth versus worn equipment without substituting texture for missing volume. If an extra limb floats or has no working range, repair its root and pose envelope; if the result reads as random noise, return to the primary transformation. Deliver anatomy/attachment section and contact/action studies, marking unverified biological or deformation assumptions.

## Alien morphology

Choose gravity/scale fiction and the character's actual interface needs: locomotion, sensing, communication and manipulation. Block contact/support and a tool/door interface before decorative eyes or surface spots. A nonhuman torso can require different camera/grip landmarks; record them rather than forcing human rig names. Separate skin/chitin/membrane materials with meaningful thickness and test a neutral three-quarter turn. If alien identity is only skin color, revise primary topology/silhouette. If proportions prevent the requested task, repair the owned interface/design explicitly. Deliver functional pose sheet and human-scale comparison; no biological or rig-compatibility claim.

## Cybernetic body

Choose which systems are replaced and where organic/synthetic transitions occur. Build socket, seal, fastening/service access and flexible connection at each transition; do not merely draw glowing seams on skin. Keep cables outside joint crush zones and plates clear of bend envelopes. Neutral light should distinguish skin, coating and bare metal; emission is a separate status layer. If the elbow locks visually, split plates/reposition flex sections. Deliver cover-on/off and bent/rest views plus interface landmarks; motor/control function remains unimplemented unless separately built.

## Robot and android

Decide whether mechanical construction is visible or human mimicry is the goal. Robots need articulated joints, bearings/clearance, covers and service openings; androids need deliberate skin/face transitions and expression compatibility. Use rigidly attached parts where appropriate instead of smooth skinning every plate. Compare opaque/emission-off silhouettes and joint extremes with a consistent scale camera. If the robot is a featureless suit, add meaningful separation/service logic; if the android becomes uncanny unintentionally, inspect face proportions/material scale before grading. Deliver mechanical/skin layer map, rest/bend/face states and explicit rig handoff.

## Mecha and pilot scale

Lock mech height and pilot/operator size independently. Block cockpit/control/maintenance access, feet/contact and main limb swept volumes before armor fins. Include a pilot proxy near the hatch and service ladder so scale survives a hero shot. Separate frame, actuators and armor; test knee/hip/shoulder clearance with an exploded diagram. If it reads as a human-sized robot, revise hatch/handrail/panel scale cues; if armor intersects, repair the local envelope rather than shrinking the pilot. Use broad coating and restrained metal/emission under neutral and dramatic light. Deliver pilot-access, service and pose sheets; walking balance or structural feasibility is not established.

## Chibi body treatment

Treat chibi as a proportion/style choice compatible with any individual role. Set head/body ratio and actual height, then preserve readable hands, feet and face at minimum screen size. Build tool grips and seated/bent states because a large head/short arm can obstruct reach. Use broad material/value groups and simple highlights; fine surface noise usually competes with the silhouette. If the head clips shoulders or props, adjust local shapes/pose range within the approved style. Deliver equal-scale proportion sheet and thumbnail expressions; do not claim adult/child age from head ratio alone.

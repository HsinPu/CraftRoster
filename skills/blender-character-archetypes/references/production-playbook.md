# Character Archetype Direction: production playbook

## Brief, scale and evidence

Define individual identity, occupation, morphology, movement and user-authored culture. An ancestry does not mandate morality, costume or physique. Establish whether this is a concept blockout or deformable production character and choose smallest intended screen size.

## Asset kit and construction

Proportion mannequin, three silhouette alternatives, body masses, role-defining tools, grip proxy and costume-layer diagram. Add joint/contact studies for nonhuman bodies and pilot/service scale for mecha when relevant.

Block torso, pelvis and limb ratios before armor. Separate rigid plates from deforming skin/cloth. Attach tools at named landmarks and keep articulation clear. Inspect neutral materials before spectral transparency or emission.

## Bounded layout recipe

These dimensions are a starting study, not established game rules.

1. Set front, side and three-quarter cameras at equal scale. Make three candidates with distinct mass distributions, not merely different hats.
2. Choose using occupation, reach and silhouette. Add one main tool and secondary role cue, then check a bent or seated proxy.
3. Place armor, holsters, wings or covers around grip/shoulder/waist landmarks; compare relaxed and working poses.
4. Render the family lineup at minimum screen height and retain editable alternate proportions and equipment.

## Diagnostic images and acceptance

Black silhouettes, orthographic ratios, joint-clearance close-ups and grayscale lineup. Add wing-open/closed, mech-access or transparency diagnostics only for that family.

## Specific failures and repairs

Color-only roles: alter mass and tool silhouette. Ground-intersecting weapon: adjust attachment/tool length rather than whole-body scale. Armor bridging elbow: split plates and inspect bent proxy. Unsupported cultural caricature: return to the individual brief.

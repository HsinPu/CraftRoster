# Medium and edge hierarchy

## Choose the medium

Oil emphasizes grouped opaque strokes and selective impasto; watercolor emphasizes luminous paper, overlapping washes and pigment accumulation; gouache can use opaque matte blocks. Read the requested reference and choose one before texturing. Painterly is an image language, distinct from merely painting a PBR albedo.

## Production recipe

Start with a grayscale composition and a limited set of large value shapes. Simplify geometry that creates accidental tiny shadows. Place crisp edges near focus and softer/lost edges elsewhere through paint, material or compositing masks rather than blurring the entire frame.

For oil, author broad form-following stroke directions in color and restrained normal/roughness variation. A low bump strength comparison around 0.05–0.15 is a starting shader experiment, not a physical paint-thickness claim. Keep stroke scale consistent with canvas/output size. For watercolor, reserve bright paper regions, use transparent-looking color layers and darker pigment edges selectively; a paper texture should remain weaker than subject forms. Avoid metallic specular that contradicts a matte wash unless the reference deliberately combines media. Gouache needs flatter opaque regions with selected dry-brush edges.

Light the scene to support painted masses, often with a broad source and restrained specular. Keep canvas/paper overlays adjustable and compare with them bypassed. Animated work needs surface-attached marks or a declared screen-space treatment so stroke swimming is not accidental.

## Repair and acceptance

Filter-like noise needs larger directional mark groups. Plastic-looking oil needs highlight restraint and stroke-following variation. Dirty watercolor needs restored paper lights and less blanket multiply.

Require value study, overlay-off/final images and a final-size stroke/edge crop. Accept a recognizable medium, intentional focus edges and marks describing form. For motion view the clip to judge temporal coherence; stills cannot establish stable strokes.
